# Forms of Engerl-Bengerl, Wichteln and Secret Santa

As of November 2026. This English version is the reference; a maintained German version lives in [`variants.de.md`](variants.de.md).

Engerl-Bengerl is the Austrian name of the custom: the Engerl (little angel) secretly gives a gift to their Bengerl (little rascal). In Germany the same custom is called Wichteln, in English-speaking countries Secret Santa. This project always puts Engerl-Bengerl first.

The custom comes in two families, and the app treats them separately. In drawn forms, names are drawn secretly in advance to decide who gives to whom (Engerl-Bengerl, Wichteln, Secret Santa). In party games, gifts go anonymously onto a table and are handed out at the gathering by a game rule (Räuberwichteln, Würfelwichteln, White Elephant). There is no assignment in advance, and the app only runs the game on one device.

Every concrete form can be described as a combination of categories A to K in section 4. Section 5 maps each form to its categories and app features.

All texts are machine-generated. The maintainer reviews the English and German versions; all other languages are unchecked. Corrections by native speakers are welcome.

For the app, role names and occasion are just labels on the same core. Engerl and Bengerl, Wichtel and Wichtelkind, Santa and giftee mean the same thing. The occasion is free: St. Nicholas, Christmas, a birthday, Easter or a team event.

Side note: in parts of New Jersey and Pennsylvania the custom is called Pollyanna and is not tied to Christmas. The standard round covers that with its free choice of occasion.

## 2. Descriptions in the original language

### Engerl-Bengerl (German, Austria)
> Vor Weihnachten zieht jede Person geheim einen Namen. Als Engerl beschenkt sie ihr Bengerl, ohne sich zu verraten; aufgelöst wird meist bei der Feier.

English: Before Christmas, everyone secretly draws a name. As the Engerl, they give a gift to their Bengerl without giving themselves away; the reveal usually happens at the party.

### Engele-Bengele (German, South Tyrol)
> Wie Engerl-Bengerl. Oft wird auch das Budget gelost: Alle schreiben einen Betrag auf einen Zettel, und der gezogene Betrag gilt für alle.

English: Like Engerl-Bengerl. Often the budget is drawn too: everyone writes an amount on a slip, and the amount drawn applies to all.

### Wichteln (German)
> Jede Person zieht heimlich einen Namen und beschenkt diese Person als Wichtel. Der Name kommt vom Wichtel, einem Hausgeist, der heimlich Gutes tut.

English: Everyone secretly draws a name and gives that person a gift as their Wichtel. The name comes from the Wichtel, a house spirit who secretly does good deeds.

### Julklapp (Svenska)
> Ordet julklapp kommer från en gammal sed: man knackade på dörren, kastade in en inslagen gåva och sprang iväg innan någon hann se vem det var. Ofta följde ett rim med paketet.

English: The word comes from an old custom of knocking on the door, throwing in a wrapped gift and running off before anyone could see who it was. A rhyme often came with the parcel.

### Secret Santa (English)
> Everyone draws a name in secret and buys that person a gift, usually within a set budget. The giver stays anonymous until the exchange, or for good.

### Kris Kringle, Kris Kindle (English, Ireland)
> In Ireland, Kris Kindle means drawing names for a gift exchange at work or in the family. The name comes from the Austrian Christkindl.

### Amigo invisible, amigo secreto (Español)
> Cada persona saca un papel con un nombre y le hace un regalo en secreto. En España se dice amigo invisible; en gran parte de Latinoamérica, amigo secreto.

English: Everyone draws a slip with a name and secretly gives that person a gift. Spain says amigo invisible; much of Latin America says amigo secreto.

### Amigo secreto, amigo oculto (Português, Brasil)
> Cada participante sorteia um nome e compra um presente. Na festa, quem começa descreve o seu amigo secreto com pistas até o grupo adivinhar; quem recebe o presente revela o próximo.

English: The reveal is a guessing chain. Whoever was given a gift goes next and describes their own Bengerl with clues.

### Angelito (Español, República Dominicana)
> Se sortean los nombres en secreto y cada persona le hace un regalo a quien le tocó. En la fiesta se descubre quién le regaló a quién.

English: Names are drawn in secret and everyone gives a gift to the person they drew. At the party, everyone finds out who gave to whom.

### Lootjes trekken (Nederlands)
> Rond Sinterklaas trekt iedereen een lootje met een naam. Je koopt een cadeau, verpakt het vaak als surprise en schrijft er een gedicht bij over de ontvanger.

English: The gift often comes in a handmade wrapping (a "surprise"), together with a poem about the recipient.

### Mikołajki (Polski)
> Na Mikołajki w szkołach i w pracy losuje się imiona. Każdy przygotowuje drobny prezent dla wylosowanej osoby.

English: On St. Nicholas Day, schools and workplaces draw names, and everyone prepares a small gift for the person they drew.

### Таємний Миколай (Українська)
> Перед Днем святого Миколая учасники таємно витягують імена і готують подарунок для людини, яка їм випала. Хто дарує, залишається таємницею.

English: Before St. Nicholas Day, participants secretly draw names and prepare a gift for the person they drew. The giver stays secret.

### Monito-Monita (Filipino)
> Bumubunot ang bawat isa ng pangalan at palihim na nireregaluhan ang nabunot. Kadalasan may tema bawat linggo bago ang Christmas party, gaya ng "something sweet" o "something soft". Sa party, ibinubunyag kung sino ang monito o monita ng bawat isa.

English: In the weeks before the party there is often a theme for each week. Monito and monita are the masculine and feminine forms.

### NZ Secret Santa (English, New Zealand)
> A nationwide game among strangers, organised online. Everyone is matched with someone they don't know and sends a gift by post.

## 3. Named variants

### 3.1 Drawn forms (names drawn in advance)

| Variant | How it works | What the app must support |
|---|---|---|
| Classic (Engerl-Bengerl, Wichteln, Secret Santa) | Draw a name, one gift within the budget, exchange at the party | draw, budget, date, private draw |
| Schrottwichteln | Buy nothing. Only give things you already own and no longer need: leftovers, bad buys, kitsch. Funny yes, broken or gross no | rule "buy nothing" in the draw, no budget |
| Advent or hidden Engerl | Over Advent, secretly slip several small gifts or good deeds, for example once a week; reveal at the party | period, rhythm, theme per week, reveal date, calendar export |
| Advent calendar | The group fills 24 doors together; each person packs 24/n parcels for one or more recipients | k gifts per person, splitting 24 slots |
| Poem or riddle | The tag carries a poem instead of a name, and the group guesses the recipient (as with lootjes trekken) | reveal type "guessing" |
| Guess the giver | The gift comes with a letter, and the recipient guesses who sent it | reveal type "guessing" |
| Guessing chain | Whoever received a gift goes next and describes their own Bengerl with clues (as with the Brazilian amigo secreto) | reveal type "chain" |
| Task | The recipient first completes a task (bake cookies, tidy the coat rack) | task text in the draw |
| Theme | Gifts follow a theme: red, local, books, edible, homemade | theme for everyone or drawn per person |
| Sustainable | Second-hand, homemade or sustainably produced; buying is allowed, unlike Schrottwichteln | theme template |
| Wish list | Wishes and no-gos go only to one's own Engerl | hint per person, collected before the draw |
| Multiple gifts | Everyone gives to k people and receives k gifts | k-regular matching |
| Conspiracy Santa | Everyone except one person pools resources to give that person a gift, in parallel for every participant | n−1 to 1 matching, cost splitting |
| Teams or groups | Teams give to teams, or nobody draws within their own household or team | groups as units or as exclusions |
| Charity | The group fulfils wish lists of children or people in need | givers only, external recipients |
| Postal or remote | Online draw, gifts sent by post, unwrapping on a video call | address only in the Engerl's own draw |
| Among strangers | Community events, for example in forums or like NZ Secret Santa | sign-up, replacement on drop-out |
| Children, school, kindergarten | Very small budget, simple gifts | slip and print mode, no personal data |

### 3.2 Party games (no assignment in advance)

| Variant | How it works | What the app must support |
|---|---|---|
| Schrottwichteln as a dice game | As above, buy nothing; gifts are handed out at the party by dice rules | dice table, timer |
| Random Wichteln | Buy a gift for an unknown person; names are drawn only at the party | live draw on one device |
| Räuberwichteln, White Elephant, Yankee Swap, Dirty Santa | In turn, open a new parcel or steal an opened one; Dirty Santa allows several rounds of stealing | turn order, steal limit, lock after n steals |
| Würfelwichteln (dice game) | Gifts lie in the middle, dice rules decide taking, swapping and passing, a timer ends the game | dice table, timer, digital die |
| Money pot | A gift at a fixed price plus a contribution to a pot; in random order, players choose a gift, the money or a place in the draw for leftover gifts | turn order, three options, final draw |
| Spider web | Strings of yarn lead through the room to the gifts | analogue only |

A typical dice table, freely editable: a 6 takes a gift from the middle. A 1 swaps with anyone, a 2 passes all gifts clockwise, 3 and 4 swap with the left or right neighbour, and a 5 picks two people who swap. Common additions are reversing the direction, skipping a turn, a double swap and a drinking rule. The game ends when the timer runs out (about 15 minutes) or when everyone has unwrapped.

### 3.3 English-language variants in the original

White Elephant:
> Everyone brings one wrapped gift. In turn, players either open a new gift or steal one that has already been opened.

Yankee Swap:
> Much like White Elephant, but the gifts are meant to be useful. The name is most common in New England.

Dirty Santa:
> White Elephant with more stealing: gifts can change hands over several rounds.

Conspiracy Santa:
> Everyone except one person secretly teams up to buy that person a gift. A separate conspiracy runs for every participant at the same time.

## 4. Categories

### A: Occasion and period
A single date, a period with a rhythm (Advent, weekly) or recurring every year. Plus dates for the draw, the exchange and the reveal.

### B: Participants
Normally everyone gives and receives. Exceptions are givers only (charity) or people who only receive (children in the family). Households or teams can count as a unit. The organizer takes part or not. People can drop out or join after the draw.

### C: Structure of the matching
- Derangement: nobody draws themselves
- No mutual pairs: A and B never give to each other (default)
- One big chain: A gives to B, B to C, all the way back to A; needed for the chain reveal
- k gifts per person
- Many to one (Conspiracy), group to group, separate givers and recipients

### D: Rules and exclusions
Couples, households and siblings do not draw each other, nor do a manager and their team; a rule can apply in one direction or both. Must rules ("A gives to B" or "A gives to someone from B and C"). No pairing from last year. Same country for postal rounds. If a combination of rules cannot be satisfied, the app says why.

### E: Secrecy
The Engerl knows their Bengerl. The Bengerl never learns their Engerl, learns it at the reveal, or is meant to guess. The organizer sees the assignment only through a deliberate hurdle. Names are drawn on paper slips, on a phone passed around or via private links.

### F: Gift rules
Budget fixed, as a cap, as a range, drawn (Engele-Bengele) or none (Schrottwichteln). Theme free, given, per week or drawn. Kind of gift: bought, homemade, an experience, a good deed, only things you already own (Schrott), second-hand. Hints such as wishes, no-gos, sizes or allergies go to the Engerl. The hint is free text; the app reminds people that no diagnoses are needed ("no nuts" is enough).

### G: Exchange
In person at the party, secretly hidden (Advent), by post (address only for the Engerl) or digitally.

### H: Communication during the round
None, anonymous questions, anonymous thanks. Without a server there is no channel after the draw; hints are therefore collected beforehand and go into the draw.

### I: Reveal
None, in person at the exchange, guessing, chain (also as a guessing chain) or stage (pair by pair on the organizer's device).

### J: Party game parameters
Turn order (random, youngest first), steal limit, lock, dice table, timer, direction.

### K: Context
Family, friends, office or team, club, school or kindergarten, online community, charity. Plus language, suitability for children (no accounts, no data) and accessibility.

## 5. Mapping forms to categories and features

Every form brings the basic set of its family and needs additional categories and features on top.

| Family | Base categories | Base features |
|---|---|---|
| Drawn forms | B, C, E, K | preset wizard, participants and households, matching engine, distribution |
| Party games | B, J, K | preset wizard, participants, turn order |

| Form | Additional categories | Additional features |
|---|---|---|
| Engerl-Bengerl classic | A, D, F, G, I | rule editor, draw contents, dates and .ics, reveal, replacement on drop-out |
| Schrottwichteln | A, D, F, I | as classic, draw contents with the rule "buy nothing" |
| Advent or hidden Engerl, Monito-Monita | A, F, G, I | dates and .ics, theme per week, reveal |
| Advent calendar | A, C, F | engine (k gifts), dates, draw contents |
| Poem or riddle, lootjes trekken | E, I | reveal "guessing" |
| Guess the giver | E, H, I | reveal "guessing" |
| Guessing chain, amigo secreto | E, I | reveal "chain" |
| Task, theme, sustainable | F | draw contents |
| Wish list | F, H | draw contents (hint) |
| Multiple gifts | C | engine (k gifts) |
| Conspiracy Santa | B, C, F | engine, draw contents |
| Teams or groups | B, C, D | participants, rule editor, engine |
| Charity | B, C, F, K | engine, draw contents |
| Postal or remote | D, G, H | draw contents (address), distribution |
| Among strangers, NZ Secret Santa | B, D, K | participants, replacement on drop-out, distribution |
| Children and school | E, F, K | distribution (slips), draw contents |
| Schrottwichteln as a dice game | F, J | dice and timer |
| Random Wichteln | A, G | engine, distribution at the party |
| Räuberwichteln | J | turn order, steal counter |
| Würfelwichteln | A, J | dice and timer |
| Money pot | F, J | turn order, engine (final draw) |
| Spider web | G | none |

Counting it out, draw contents and the matching engine carry almost every form. Dice, timer and steal counter are needed only for party games. That sets the order of implementation in the concept.

## 6. Templates for simple use

To cover everything and still stay simple, the breadth hides behind templates. Everything else sits under "More options" and never in the main flow. "No mutual pairs" is on in every template and can be turned off.

| Template | Family | Defaults |
|---|---|---|
| Engerl-Bengerl classic | drawn | households excluded, budget, exchange date, reveal at the party |
| Schrottwichteln | drawn | no budget, rule "buy nothing, only what you already have" in the draw |
| Family Wichteln | drawn | households excluded, no pairing from last year, hints per person |
| Advent Engerl | drawn | Advent period, once a week, optional theme per week, reveal date |
| Office Wichteln | drawn | theme optional, organizer takes part |
| Postal Wichteln | drawn | address only in the Engerl's draw |
| Advent calendar | drawn | k gifts per person, 24 slots |
| Räuberwichteln | party | random turn order, steal limit |
| Würfelwichteln | party | standard dice table, 15-minute timer, optionally as Schrottwichteln |
| Random Wichteln | party | live draw at the party |
